# Hypercomb launch kit

## One sentence

Hypercomb is free, open-source, community-owned software where your work lives
on your device and published content travels by verifiable signature.

## Twenty-second explanation

Most software keeps your work behind somebody else's account on somebody
else's servers. Hypercomb starts differently: your hive lives with you, no
account is required, and anything you publish is named by its own content so
the receiver can verify every byte. It is free, open source, and designed to
be forked by the communities that use it.

## Launch post

I built Hypercomb because I wanted software that did not ask a company for
permission to remember, organize or share my work.

Your hive lives on your device. There is no account. Published content is
named by its own bytes, so whoever receives it can verify it. Features are
signed modules a community can adopt or fork for itself.

Hypercomb is free, open source, and not for sale. I have funded its development
myself. I am opening a voluntary support channel for people who want to help me
continue the work and recover part of what I put into getting it here. Support

## Show HN draft

**Title:** Show HN: Hypercomb – local-first, accountless, community-owned software

Hypercomb is a browser-based workspace built from nested hexagonal cells. A
hive starts in the browser's private file system; there is no account. Content
is addressed by SHA-256 over canonical bytes, and published branches move as
signed, Merkle-composed artifacts. Features are signed modules loaded by a
small shell.

The goal is practical community ownership: a community should be able to carry
its work and capabilities, verify what it is running, and fork the software
without losing access to its hive.

Working app: https://hypercomb.io

Narrated tour: https://hypercomb.com/tour/

Source (AGPL-3.0): https://github.com/hypercomb/social

I would especially value feedback on first-run comprehension, the local-first
storage model, and the signature-addressed module boundary.

## Non-invasive distribution rules

- Post only where project sharing is permitted and follow each community's
  self-promotion rules.
- Never scrape addresses, buy lists or followers, mass-message strangers, or
  manufacture votes and endorsements.
- Do not use tracking pixels or retargeting profiles.
- Make every post independently useful; do not repeat the same pitch across
  unrelated discussions.
- Ask once, accept silence, and make opting out unnecessary by not enrolling
  people in the first place.
