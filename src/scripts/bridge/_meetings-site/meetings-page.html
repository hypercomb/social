Hypercomb · beginner meetings

Six evenings from your first hive to your first bee.

A hands-on night for people who make things and are tired of losing them in folders. Bring a laptop, nothing to install. A ten-minute live demo, then you build in a shared hive with a bee guiding you and a buddy beside you.

90 minutes a night 8 to 15 people Free, first-timers welcome Coffee and snacks included

The six nights

One idea per night, in the order the app's own tutorial teaches them. The demo is the idea; the hands-on hour is the matching course, flown by the bee with /tutorial.

Every month
1

Welcome to the hive

Course: /tutorial start

Where did the last thing you shared actually go, and who can still see it?

Presence is permission. A hive exists while people are in it, and nothing is stored unless someone publishes it.

You leave able to join a hive from a QR code, go in and out of cells, zoom, pan and select.

Every month
2

Make things by typing

Course: /tutorial beginner

How many clicks does it take to make a folder, name it, and put a note in it?

One command line, three stances: a name creates, / commands, ? asks.

You leave able to create and edit cells, copy, cut, paste, undo, redo, fit and center.

3

Find and mean

Course: /tutorial intermediate

How do you find a thing when you forgot where you put it but remember what it was about?

A mark is a word the hive can search. A pheromone is a signal from a reader, never a label.

You leave able to filter a layer, search a branch or the whole hive, and add marks, titles and references.

4

Share by signature

Course: sharing lessons and /language

Why does the same photo exist nine times across your devices?

Same content, same signature. One hash is the name, the version and the permission.

You leave able to explain a signature, share one, pull content from a host and switch the language.

5

The windows

Course: /tutorial expert

Which window do you open to undo yesterday?

One window per job: palette, editor, notes, files, history, rewind and the assistant.

You leave able to use every primary window, rewind the night's hive and ask the assistant.

6

Build a bee

Pair build, for anyone who reads code

What is the smallest thing you could add to the app without asking anyone?

A drone senses and acts on every heartbeat and talks only through the effect bus.

You leave able to read a drone, change one effect and see the hive react.

Nights 1 and 2 run every month, so you can start any time. Nights 3 to 6 rotate, and regulars can repeat any of them. If your first visit lands on a later night, a buddy pairs with you and you fly the starter course instead.

How a night runs

The same shape every time, so regulars know the rhythm and you can arrive late without missing the point.

  1. 0:00Doors, coffee, snacksA hex name tag with a bee sticker, and a QR code on the screen that joins the evening's hive.
  2. 0:15Intro roundOne question for the room, thirty seconds each.
  3. 0:30Short demoTen minutes, one idea, live in the app. No slides: the hive is the slide.
  4. 0:45Hands-onThe bee flies the night's course while helpers roam. Everyone builds something in the shared hive.
  5. 1:15Show-and-tellWhat people built, then free mingling by the food.
  6. 1:30CloseOne takeaway each and next night's topic. The hive goes away with the people, as promised.

Fun, food and people

People are bees, the room is the hive, and nothing is stored unless someone publishes it.

Icebreakers in the app

  • Hive bingo. A grid of cells on the big screen, filled with each other's names, live.
  • Six-neighbour chain. Add a cell next to the last person's, naming something you share.
  • Signature match. The first table to find the content behind a signature picks the music.
  • Pheromone vote. Mark the demo you liked most; the winner presents first next time.

Intro questions

  1. Name, what you make, and one thing you would put in a hive tonight.
  2. What is the last thing you lost in a folder and never found again?
  3. What did you build last month that nobody saw?
  4. Describe your job in three cells, drawn live.

Why people come back

  • Buddy bees. Every first-timer is paired with a regular for the hands-on hour.
  • Lightning slot. Five minutes for one member to show what they built since last time.
  • A shared contact cell, published only if the room says yes.
  • Honey on the table: coffee, honey-lemon tea, something sweet and something savoury.
