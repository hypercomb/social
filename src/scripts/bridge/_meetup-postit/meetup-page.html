Social Networking for Humanity · Vancouver

Meet Hypercomb — a social network you own, explore, and build together

What if your corner of the internet actually belonged to you? A friendly, beginner-level introduction, every Saturday morning in Yaletown.

Hypercomb is a new kind of social network being built right here in Vancouver. There's no feed, no algorithm, and no company sitting between you and your community. Instead, you build a hive — a honeycomb of hexagonal tiles that hold your pictures, notes, links, collections, even whole games and websites — and it lives on your own device. You choose what to publish and share; nobody harvests the rest.

Explore, don't scrollYou find things by panning and zooming across honeycombs people have built — the way you'd wander a neighbourhood, not the way a feed is fed to you.
Yours, on your deviceYour hive is files you hold, not rows in someone's database. Publishing is a choice, tile by tile.
Nothing is ever lostEvery hive carries its complete history — rewind it to any moment.
Communities swarmFind a hive you love? Adopt it, make it your own, improve it, pass it on — hives grow by splitting and sharing, like real ones.

What to expect

A relaxed, hands-on introduction for newcomers — people curious about community and connection, intrigued by Hypercomb's vision, or simply interested in a more meaningful way for communities to form online. No technical experience needed. Everything is introduced step by step, with plenty of time for questions, discussion, and hands-on exploration. You're not just listening — you're helping shape how this new model is understood.

Agenda

  1. Welcome & introductions — who's in the room and what brings you here.
  2. What is Hypercomb? — why it exists, the hive-and-tile idea, and how it differs from feed-based social media: you own your data, there are no ads or algorithms, and discovery happens by exploration.
  3. How hives work — how tiles appear and connect, how a hive grows and reorganizes as people participate, and why exploring beats being fed.
  4. Live demo — watch a hive come to life on screen: tiles placed, notes added, a collection becoming a small shareable website. This is where it clicks.
  5. Group discussion — what would you build? Local groups, classrooms, family archives, projects, businesses…
  6. Getting involved — how to try it yourself, join future sessions, and help shape Hypercomb while it's still early.
